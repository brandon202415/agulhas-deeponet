### Issue 1 — Physics-informed test may be circular by construction
**Reviewer's argument:** GLORYS12V1 is itself NEMO (physics-based GCM) output. Testing whether a divergence-free/geostrophic soft penalty helps a network trained to reproduce a physics model's own output was close to guaranteed to find the penalty inert. The manuscript spends Sec. 3.2–3.4 and all of Sec. 4.4 treating the null result as a rigorously earned, mechanistically diagnosed finding, when a meaningful part of it was predictable in advance from the data source alone — this is currently disclosed only in Limitations point 1, as an afterthought rather than a framing that should shape how the whole Aim 1/2 result is read.

**What fixing this requires:** No new experiment needed — this is a narrative-balance problem, not a missing-data problem. The manuscript already contains the honest version of this point (Limitations #1: "the negative physics result is specific to training on physics-consistent reanalysis... may behave differently on sparser or noisier observational data"). The fix is moving that acknowledgment earlier and into the result's own framing — Sec. 4.4's "mechanistically clear reason" language currently reads as if the diagnostic work discovered something surprising, when the surprising part (the constraints are inert) was foreseeable from knowing GLORYS is GCM output before running anything. Candidate fix: add a sentence to the Introduction's hypothesis statement (Sec. 1) flagging this risk *before* presenting the result, and soften "mechanistically clear reason" framing in 4.4 and the abstract's Second-finding sentence to acknowledge the predictability question directly rather than only in Limitations.

**Literature context (2026-07-28):** A broad PINN soft-constraint-critique literature exists (gradient pathologies, no guaranteed constraint satisfaction, need for careful weight tuning — Wang et al. 2021, Krishnapriyan et al. 2021, plus a "hard-constraint" architectural-fix literature), but that thread is about general optimization pathology, not this paper's specific mechanism (constraint already satisfied by physics-consistent *labels*, so no gradient to supply). No paper was found making that exact "reanalysis is itself physics-model output, so a penalty on top of it is circular" argument explicitly for ocean-reanalysis-trained networks. **This reverses the recommended fix direction:** rather than only softening/conceding the framing, the paper should lean into this as one of its actual citable, novel contributions — a clean, generalizable methodological point (be careful applying physics penalties when "ground truth" is itself a physics model's output) that appears to be underexplored in print. The text-only fix above (move the acknowledgment earlier, don't bury it in Limitations) still applies, but the *tone* should shift from "concession" to "stated finding," e.g. promoting it toward its own framing in the Introduction and Discussion rather than only softening Sec. 4.4's confidence.

**Status:** Fixed (2026-07-29). Text-only edit to both `Agulhas_DeepONet_Manuscript.md` and `.tex`: abstract's Second-finding sentence, Sec. 1's hypothesis paragraph (now flags the risk before presenting the result), and Sec. 4.4 (retitled, body now states the foreseeability point as a generalizable methodological contribution rather than only a post hoc mechanistic explanation). PDF rebuilt cleanly. See `RESEARCH_LOG.md` item 24a.

**Carried into the restructured manuscript (2026-07-30):** condensed further per `MANUSCRIPT_RESTRUCTURE_PLAN.md` — now one Methods subsection (Sec. 2.7) stating the physics-informed null result plainly, plus one Discussion paragraph (Sec. 4.6) framing it as a citable methodological caution, rather than the multi-section ablation apparatus the original draft carried. ### Issue 2 — Patch-vs-whole-domain result (t=13.2, the paper's strongest claim) is confounded with a data-augmentation effect the authors flag but never isolate
**Reviewer's argument:** Sec. 4.9 admits outright that patch tiling "effectively multiplies the number of (day, tile) training examples available per epoch" and that "a matched-compute ablation... would be needed to confirm or rule out" this as the actual driver of patch's advantage over whole-domain — not the architecture per se. This is the single most statistically dramatic result in the paper (larger t than the physics ablation by ~40x) and the confound that could fully explain it is currently a footnote in the discussion, not something the paper has ruled out.

**What fixing this requires:** A genuine new experiment — a matched-compute ablation holding total training examples (or gradient updates) seen fixed between the patch and whole-domain configurations, then comparing skill. Concretely: either (a) cap the patch model's training to the same number of (day) samples the whole-domain model sees per epoch (i.e., don't let tiling multiply effective dataset size), or (b) give the whole-domain model an equivalent multiplier (e.g., more epochs / iterations matched to patch's effective sample count) and see if the gap closes. This is a real cluster job, not a text fix, and it's the largest single technical debt item in the list — if patch's advantage shrinks substantially under matched compute, this is the paper's headline claim and would need to be reframed throughout (abstract, Sec. 3.6, 4.9, Conclusion).

**Literature context (2026-07-28):** Domain-decomposed DeepONets are not new — DD-DeepONet (Yang et al., 2025) couples local DeepONets via Schwarz-alternating/Robin-type interface iteration for PDE solving on complex geometries, and "many small local models instead of one big one" is standard in the tiled/patch-based dense-prediction CV literature (PatchFusion, DDU-Net, Swin-style hierarchical patching) for the exact reason rediscovered here (parameter count exploding with resolution). This paper's scheme is a cruder variant with *no cross-tile interface exchange at all* — pure independent-tile training with post-hoc overlap-averaging, unlike Schwarz-type DD-DeepONet's iterative reconciliation — and should cite DD-DeepONet and situate itself accordingly rather than presenting tiling-for-scalability as unprecedented. More importantly: patch/tile training multiplying effective training examples per epoch is a **well-known, expected effect** in the patch-based vision literature, not a speculative hypothesis. This means Sec. 4.9's "our working hypothesis, not directly tested" framing understates how likely this confound is to be the real explanation — it reinforces, not just permits, that the matched-compute ablation is necessary before "patch beats whole-domain, t=13.2" is defensible as an architectural claim rather than a data-multiplication artifact.

**Status:** Open. Requires new full-scale training runs before the current "patch beats whole-domain, t=13.2" framing can be defended as stated. Literature review increases confidence the confound is real, not just possible — raises the priority of this item.

**Resolved by scope cut (2026-07-30):** the manuscript has been restructured around a CNN/double-penalty/AMSE-loss story (`MANUSCRIPT_RESTRUCTURE_PLAN.md`); the patch-vs-whole-domain DeepONet comparison this issue concerns is no longer in the paper. This is not a claim the matched-compute confound has been resolved — it remains exactly as open as before — only that it no longer needs resolving *for this manuscript*. The full result (including this open confound) remains documented in `RESEARCH_LOG.md` as candidate material for a separate DeepONet-architecture paper. See `RESEARCH_LOG.md` item 37.

**Related exploratory attempt (2026-07-28): DD-DeepONet interface-consistency variant, built and locally screened, on hold.** Prompted by the DD-DeepONet literature above, built `train_agulhas_deeponet_patch_dd.py` — adds a soft interface-consistency penalty (Robin-type, not full Schwarz alternation, which doesn't map onto a one-shot forecasting task) between a tile and a randomly sampled overlapping neighbor each training step, on top of the base patch trainer's existing per-tile supervision. Verified correct (`--dd-weight 0` reproduces the base patch trainer's exact parameter count and architecture; the interface term computes and moves sensibly during training). Local weight sweep (single seed, 3000 iterations, dd_weight ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.1, 1.0}) was flat/inconclusive — all four landed within +0.054 to +0.058 mean skill, no monotonic trend, likely within seed noise. This is a different signal from the two other cross-tile mechanisms already tried in this study (cross-tile attention showed a clear *negative* local signal that justified ruling it out before any full-scale investment; multi-day history showed a clear *positive* local signal that justified promoting it to full scale) — flat doesn't rule this out the way attention's negative result did, but doesn't justify full-scale investment yet either, since the local 700-day dataset may simply be too small for a data-efficiency-motivated mechanism like this to show its effect (full scale has 10x the training days). **Held at user's request** pending the CNN 5-seed confirmation and other higher-priority items; code is complete and ready to resume (local sweep, then a full-scale weight sweep + multi-seed confirmation mirroring the CNN baseline's pattern) whenever prioritized.

### Issue 3 — No non-persistence learned baseline anywhere in the paper
**Reviewer's argument:** Every headline skill number is "beats persistence," never "beats a CNN" or "beats a U-Net." Given how modest the raw effect sizes are (+0.043 mean skill ≈ 2% RMSE reduction over tomorrow-equals-today), and that ocean-ML papers routinely include a simple CNN baseline, this is a meaningful gap — without it there's no way to tell whether DeepONet's branch-trunk formulation is doing anything distinctive versus "well-tuned local model + persistence-residual skip + correct LR" reproducing the same result with any reasonable architecture.

**What fixing this requires:** A CNN/U-Net baseline, trained and evaluated to the *same* rigor standard as the paper's central claims (multi-seed, full-scale, same split, same skill/NRMSE/ACC metrics). Per `RESEARCH_LOG.md`, a CNN baseline was previously attempted and smoke-tested (early snapshot: mean skill +0.18 at 200 iterations) but the full run was killed twice by local interruptions and "never reached a completed, trustworthy result" — this work exists in a half-finished state and could be resumed on the cluster rather than started from scratch. This is a substantial but well-scoped new experiment.

**Status:** Confirmed at single-seed scale (2026-07-28) — the reviewer's suspicion was correct, and the gap is large. `train_cnn_baseline.py` completed (rollout evaluation mirroring `rollout_evaluate()` exactly, NRMSE/bias parity with Table 1, `--loss-weight` flag) and verified end-to-end (local smoke tests + full tooling-chain check with `aggregate_seed_sweep.py`/`extract_rollout_rmse.py`). `cnn_lr_sweep.slurm` ran on the cluster, full scale, r=6, single seed (2026), same 3 learning rates as Table 2:

| | DeepONet (Table 1, its own winning config) | CNN, best LR (1e-3) | CNN, lr3e-4 | CNN, lr1e-4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean skill | +0.043 | **+0.379** | +0.340 | +0.302 |
| Params | 14,239,206 | 433,734 (33x smaller) | — | — |

Every one of the 3 tested CNN configs beats the DeepONet's own best result by 7-9x on mean skill, and the gap holds per-variable (e.g. `vo`: DeepONet +0.122 vs. CNN +0.667) and under rollout — the CNN's `10d`/`20d` rollout skill stays positive or near-zero at every LR (`lr1e-4`: +0.192 at 10d, **+0.073 at 20d**) while the DeepONet's Table 3 decays to -0.069 by 20d and never recovers. A plausible, non-alarming mechanism: the CNN is 33x smaller and uses real 2D convolutions (local receptive field, translation-equivariant) vs. the whole-domain DeepONet's dense-MLP branch reading the entire flattened domain with no spatial locality prior — consistent with, and a much stronger version of, the same lesson the paper's own patch-DeepONet result already suggested (locality helps: patch +0.075 vs. whole-domain +0.043). ### Issue 4 — The one test of DeepONet's actual namesake claim (discretization invariance) is a hastily patched, single-seed, local-prototype check
**Reviewer's argument:** Sec. 3.7 required manually bypassing the model's own forward pass (a shape error otherwise) to test zero-shot resolution transfer, at n_train=700, one seed, one resolution pair. The patch architecture's resolution flexibility — the paper's strongest result — comes from *retraining* at each tile configuration, not continuous-coordinate querying, so the actual discretization-invariance property that motivated choosing DeepONet over a CNN in Sec. 1 is demonstrated only by this one degraded, unreplicated check. If this is the extent of support for DeepONet's core architectural rationale, the paper owes readers the harder question of why DeepONet at all versus a simpler local CNN.

**What fixing this requires:** Two possible directions, not mutually exclusive: (a) rerun the existing zero-shot resolution-transfer check at full scale, multi-seed, and ideally more than one resolution pair, still using the forward-pass workaround; (b) the more architecturally honest fix — reformulate the persistence-residual term so it's computed at the query resolution rather than sliced from the fixed-size branch input (already identified as the needed change in Limitations #10: "an out-of-the-box discretization-invariant inference path would need the residual term reformulated... not yet implemented"), removing the need for the workaround entirely. (b) is a real architecture change plus validation; (a) alone is a rerun of existing code at larger scale.

**Literature context (2026-07-28) — the single most important finding of the whole literature review.** Recent work directly interrogates whether DeepONet-family operators actually deliver zero-shot resolution transfer, and the answer is already established, specifically for DeepONet, as no:
- "The False Promise of Zero-Shot Super-Resolution in Machine-Learned Operators" (2025) tests DeepONet specifically and states plainly that it "does not claim to perform accurate zero-shot super- and sub-resolution" and "operates at a fixed resolution on the input and is only resolution independent on the output" — i.e. the trunk (output query) is continuous, but the branch (input sensor grid) is not. This is *exactly* the architectural snag Sec. 3.7 discovers by hand (the residual-slicing shape error) — the paper rediscovers empirically, at n=700 and one seed, something already established as an architectural fact.
- "Is Zero-Shot Super-Resolution Possible in Operator Learning?" (2026) proves an impossibility theorem: benign operators exist for which no estimator, including DeepONet and FNO, achieves vanishing error at finer test resolution than training — documenting the empirical failure pattern generally, not just for this paper's case.
- Raonić et al. (2023) attribute DeepONet/FNO zero-shot resolution failures specifically to a lack of translation equivariance, and show translation-equivariant convolutional neural operators do better — directly relevant given Issue 3's CNN result (translation-equivariant, and dramatically better).

**This changes how Sec. 3.7 and Sec. 1 should be framed, not just how rigorously Sec. 3.7 should be validated.** The paper currently presents its weak (+0.022, half of native skill, single-seed, hand-patched) zero-shot result as a novel finding "this study tests directly, rather than assumes." It should instead be framed as a confirmatory data point in an already-documented pattern: discretization invariance only ever applied to the output query coordinates, not the input sensor grid, and DeepONet's zero-shot resolution transfer is known to be weak/unreliable. More consequentially, **Sec. 1 motivates choosing DeepONet over a CNN partly on discretization invariance without acknowledging this is now a contested, largely-settled-negative claim in the literature** — that motivation needs revisiting regardless of whether Issue 4's own rerun/reformulation work happens, and it compounds directly with Issue 3's CNN result (see the cross-reference added there): the literature says DeepONet's namesake advantage doesn't hold on the input side, and empirically a CNN wins by an order of magnitude on this exact task. Together these substantially undercut the paper's stated rationale for the architecture choice itself, not just this one section's rigor.

**Status:** Open, but re-scoped. The rerun/reformulation work (full-scale, multi-seed, or the residual-reformulation architecture change) is still worth doing, but is now secondary to the more urgent fix: cite this literature, reframe Sec. 3.7's result as confirmatory rather than novel, and revisit Sec. 1's stated rationale for DeepONet over a CNN in light of both the literature and the empirical CNN result (Issue 3).

**Resolved by scope cut, literature-citation half done (2026-07-30):** the restructured manuscript no longer contains Sec. 3.7's zero-shot resolution-transfer check or the patch architecture that this issue's rerun/reformulation work would apply to — that material is out of scope for the new draft (`RESEARCH_LOG.md` item 37). The more urgent half of this issue's fix — citing the literature and revisiting Sec. 1's DeepONet-over-CNN rationale — is done more thoroughly than originally requested: the new draft's Introduction (Sec. 1) opens by citing exactly this literature (Raonić et al. 2023; Sakarvadia et al. 2025; Subedi & Tewari 2026) as the reason the paper uses a CNN as its primary architecture at all, rather than revisiting a DeepONet-centered rationale after the fact.

### Issue 5 — Eddy-tracking null result rests on a detection filter loosened to near-nothing (1 px vs. standard 4 px)
**Reviewer's argument:** The paper's central "grid-point skill gain doesn't translate to eddy-level improvement" claim (repeated across the abstract, Sec. 3.5, 3.6, 4.8, and the conclusion) uses py-eddy-tracker with its minimum-contour-size filter loosened to 1 pixel, down from the field-standard 4-pixel oceanographic threshold. The paper's own sensitivity check (Sec. 4.8) shows tightening to 4px collapses detectable eddies from 75 to 10 over the same 50 days — nowhere near enough sample size for the "indistinguishable from persistence" conclusion to be trustworthy at the threshold a skeptical reviewer would actually accept as standard practice.

**What fixing this requires:** Running the eddy-tracking comparison at higher native resolution (r=3, which Table 11 already shows increases eddy counts) *combined with* the standard 4px filter, to see whether sample size becomes adequate at strict-filter settings — rather than the current approach of loosening the filter to preserve sample size at coarse resolution. This is a moderate-effort rerun (the patch architecture already supports r=3; the eddy-tracking pipeline exists and would need to be pointed at r=3 patch-model output with the default filter). If the strict-filter, fine-resolution sample is still too small, that itself is a finding worth reporting explicitly rather than working around.

**Literature context (2026-07-28):** Separate from the filter-threshold statistical-power problem above, the paper frames the whole "large RMSE gain, zero eddy-tracking gain" dissociation (Sec. 3.5, 3.6, 4.8) as a novel empirical puzzle. It isn't — it's a named, well-studied phenomenon in spatial forecast verification: the **double-penalty problem** (Ebert 2008; Gilleland et al. 2009), where point-to-point metrics like RMSE penalize a correctly-shaped, correctly-timed feature twice if it's spatially displaced (once as a missed observation, once as a false prediction) — exactly the geometry of "model predicts a slightly-off eddy field but RMSE looks fine because RMSE doesn't score closed-contour identity." Cui et al. 2025 (WenHai) — this paper's own Sec. 4.7 comparison point — explicitly invokes the double-penalty issue as the reason they don't rely on point-to-point RMSE alone, using a neighborhood-based verification metric instead. OceanNet (Gulf Stream/Loop Current eddy forecasting, *Nature Sci. Reports* 2024 + companion JGR/Ocean Science papers) pairs RMSE/correlation with a modified Hausdorff distance (MHD) object-tracking metric for the same reason, on a very similar western-boundary-current-eddy problem, and is not cited. Recommended fix: cite Ebert (2008), Gilleland et al. (2009), and Roberts & Lean (2008) for the neighborhood/FSS-based remedy, frame the eddy-tracking dissociation as a domain-specific confirmation of an established phenomenon rather than an open mystery for "future work," and consider adding a neighborhood-based or FSS-style metric alongside py-eddy-tracker (the way WenHai did) as the natural next step — this is a stronger, more literature-grounded version of "try harder at eddy detection" than anything specific to the filter threshold alone. The paired bootstrap/permutation testing already in Sec. 2.7/3.5 is more rigorous than most of this literature bothers with — worth keeping and citing as exceeding the field's typical standard, not just fixing what's missing.

**Status:** Open. Two separable fixes: (a) literature citation + reframing (text-only, low effort, should happen regardless of anything else), (b) the r=3 + standard-filter rerun (moderate effort) to address statistical power specifically.
